The Periodic Table of the Human Elements

Your AI already has a definition of a human being. It just never showed it to you.

Every AI speaks to you from an idea of what you are. In most systems that idea was never chosen — it accumulated, from training data, product decisions, and engagement metrics, into something thin: a user, a set of preferences, a source of prompts, a problem to be optimized. It works. It is also wrong, and you can feel it in the answers.

This table is the alternative: the words that define a human being, defined carefully, in the classical philosophical tradition — Aristotle, Boethius, Aquinas — by reason, in plain language, for anyone who wants to check the reasoning. That includes the oldest claim of all — that a human being is a creature, created by God — which Aquinas argues philosophically, as creation: the emanation of all being from the universal cause, God (Summa Theologiae I, qq. 44–45).

The Greeks used one word, stoicheion, for both an element and a letter of the alphabet — letters were, quite literally, the elements from which everything written is built. These words — the elements of the human being — work the same way. They are the elements from which an understanding of a person is built.

God, the Creator. One thing stands behind the whole table and is not in it: God, the Creator. A human being is not self-made; every element below describes someone whose existence is received.

Select any element to open its entry. On a narrow screen, the table scrolls sideways.

Reading

How to read the table

Each element has a number, and the number is a promise: no word is used before it has been defined. The columns are families: each column groups elements of a similar kind — foundations, constitution, transcendentals, powers, relationship, action, ends. Read down a column, then move to the next. An element may build on anything above it in its own column, and on anything in the columns to its left — never on what comes below or to the right. So person — element 4, an individual substance of a rational nature — rests on the foundations at its left; dignity follows from person; and freedom — element 14 — follows from Aquinas's “dominion over his own actions,” a clause planted in person (4) and harvested there. Between Constitution and Powers stands a column of a different kind — the Transcendentals: truth, goodness, and beauty are not parts of a person or powers of a person, but the horizons every power aims at. Reason aims at truth, freedom and desire at goodness, and the heart is awakened by beauty. The Relationship column runs its full course within itself: relationship (16) — the fact that no person is complete alone — its act in love (17), willing the good of the other, and its fruit in communion (18), where love that endures becomes a shared life.

The table grows downwards: as new elements are defined, they take their place at the foot of their family column. Read in order, the table is one argument. Read any cell on its own, and it still stands — but check its number and its column, and you will know exactly which earlier words it rests on.

Caution

A necessary caution

A person is not a compound.

Nobody is assembled from these elements and nobody can be taken apart into them — the last element in the table is mystery, and it is there on purpose. These are the simplest terms from which understanding of a human being is built. The human being always exceeds the table.

For your AI

Give your AI a better definition of you

The whole table is also one file. Download it and give it to your AI — as its personality file, system prompt, or standing instructions, wherever yours accepts such a thing — and its working definition of you changes: from user to person, from preferences to dignity, from engagement to truth.

Every definition here appears twice: once for you, to contemplate and to judge — and once for the machine, as instructions it can act on.
